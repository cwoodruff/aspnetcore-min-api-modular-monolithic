## Phone Call Script

### Opening (15 seconds)

> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I specialize in technical training for .NET
> development teams. Is this a good time for a quick conversation?"

**If yes, continue. If no:**

> "No problem. I'm reaching out because I've helped teams like yours
> at [Similar Company] reduce their architecture debates by half. When would be a
> better time to chat?"

### Discovery (60 seconds)

> "I work with engineering leaders who are dealing with some common challenges:
>
> - Code reviews that turn into architecture debates
> - New hires taking months to become productive
> - Different developers doing things different ways
> - Senior people spending too much time mentoring instead of building
>
> Are any of those happening on your team?"

**Listen and probe. Common responses:**

- "Yes, our code reviews take forever"
- "Onboarding is a struggle"
- "We don't have a consistent approach"
- "My seniors are stretched thin"

**Follow-up questions:**

- "How long does it typically take a new developer to be productive on your
  team?"
- "How much time would you estimate your seniors spend on mentoring vs. feature
  work?"
- "When was the last time your team aligned on architectural patterns together?"

### Value Proposition (60 seconds)

> "What I've found is that most of these problems come from the same root cause:
> the team was never in a room together learning the same patterns at the same
> time.
>
> I've developed a one-day, hands-on workshop where your entire team builds a
> production-ready API together—learning the same patterns, the same approaches,
> and the same vocabulary.
>
> The result is that code reviews get faster because everyone knows what 'good'
> looks like. New hires ramp up quicker because there's a clear 'way we do
> things.' And your seniors can point to the workshop materials instead of
> explaining the same thing for the tenth time.
>
> A VP of Engineering I worked with recently told me their team went from '
> everyone has their own way' to 'we have a way' in one day. Code review time
> dropped 50% within a month."

### Qualifying Questions (45 seconds)

> "A few quick questions:
>
> 1. How large is your .NET development team?
> 2. Are they co-located or distributed?
> 3. What's driving the need for training right now—is there a new project, a
     reorganization, a lot of new hires?"

### The Ask (30 seconds)

**If qualified:**

> "Based on what you've shared, this sounds like a good fit. The next step would
> be a 30-minute discovery call where I learn more about your team and can put
> together a customized proposal. I have availability [Day] at [Time] or [Day]
> at [Time]—does either work?"

**If not immediately qualified:**

> "It sounds like timing might not be right at the moment. Can I send you some
> information and check back in [timeframe]?"

### Handling Objections

**"We don't have budget for training."**

> "I understand budgets are tight. Here's how other leaders have justified it:
> the workshop typically pays for itself within 6-8 weeks through reduced meeting
> time and faster onboarding. For a team of 12, we're talking
> about $14,000 total investment versus $100,000+ in annual savings. Would it help
> if I put together an ROI calculation specific to your team size?"

**"We can't take a whole day away from work."**

> "I hear that a lot. The way I think about it: your team is already spending
> time on architecture discussions, mentoring, and code review debates. This
> workshop consolidates that scattered time into one focused day, then reduces the
> ongoing time spent. Most leaders tell me they get the day back within the first
> month. Would a Tuesday-Thursday work better to avoid Monday ramp-up and Friday
> wind-down?"

**"We've done training before and it didn't stick."**

> "That's a valid concern. Most training doesn't stick because it's
> passive—watching videos or sitting through lectures. This workshop is 80%
> hands-on coding. Your team builds a working API together. They leave with code
> they wrote and reference materials they'll actually use. I also include 30 days
> of follow-up Q&A support to reinforce the learning. How was your previous
> training delivered?"

**"My team is distributed/remote."**

> "We offer virtual delivery that works well for distributed teams. We use Zoom
> or Teams with breakout rooms for exercises. It's interactive, not a webinar.
> Would virtual work better for your situation?"

**"I need to discuss with my VP/CTO."**

> "Absolutely. Would it help if I sent you a one-page summary with the ROI
> calculation that you could share? I can also join a call with them if that would
> be useful. When do you think you'd have an answer?"

**"Can we just buy the materials and do it ourselves?"**

> "We have a 'Recording Rights' option where I deliver the workshop once and you
> get a recording for future team members. Or if you have internal trainers, I
> offer a train-the-trainer option. Which would be more useful?"

### Closing

> "Great. I'll send you a calendar invite for [Date/Time] and a brief overview
> of the workshop. Before we hang up—what's the one thing you'd most want to see
> addressed for your team?"

## Quick Reference: Key Talking Points

### Pain Points to Probe

| Pain Point                 | Follow-Up Question                                                                      |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Code reviews take too long | "How much time per week does your team spend in code review debates vs. actual review?" |
| Slow onboarding            | "How long does it take a new hire to make their first meaningful contribution?"         |
| Inconsistent patterns      | "When was the last time your whole team aligned on architectural patterns?"             |
| Senior devs stretched thin | "What percentage of your seniors' time goes to mentoring vs. feature work?"             |
| Architecture decisions     | "How long does a typical architecture decision take to resolve?"                        |

### ROI Levers

1. **Time savings** — Less debate = more shipping
2. **Onboarding acceleration** — New hires productive faster
3. **Quality improvement** — Consistent patterns = fewer bugs
4. **Retention** — Developers value growth opportunities
5. **Technical debt reduction** — Right patterns from the start

### Comparison to Alternatives

| Option            | Time         | Cost            | Retention    | Team Alignment |
|-------------------|--------------|-----------------|--------------|----------------|
| Online courses    | 20+ hrs each | $50-500/person  | Low (10-20%) | None           |
| Conference talks  | 1 hr         | $500-2000       | Medium (30%) | Limited        |
| Books/docs        | 10+ hrs each | $50/person      | Low (10%)    | None           |
| **This Workshop** | 8 hrs total  | $500-700/person | High (70%+)  | Complete       |

### Closing Line

> "The difference is simple: hands-on practice together beats passive learning
> alone. Your team leaves with code they wrote, patterns they understand, and a
> shared vocabulary for every architecture discussion going forward."
