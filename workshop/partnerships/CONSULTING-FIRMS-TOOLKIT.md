## Phone Call Script

### Opening (15 seconds)

> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name]. I help consulting firms add technical
> training to their service offerings. Do you have a couple of minutes?"

**If yes, continue. If no:**

> "No problem. When's a better time? I have something that's helped firms like
> yours create a new revenue stream while strengthening client relationships."

### Discovery (60 seconds)

> "I work with IT consulting firms who are looking for ways to differentiate
> from competitors and expand their service offerings.
>
> Two things I hear a lot:
> 1. Clients are asking for training as part of engagements, but you don't have
     curriculum ready
> 2. Your own consultants need upskilling, but building training internally
     takes too long
>
> Does either of those resonate with what you're experiencing?"

**Listen for pain points. Common responses:**

- "We've had clients ask for training"
- "We need to upskill our team on modern .NET"
- "We want to differentiate from other consultancies"

### Value Proposition (45 seconds)

> "I've developed a full-day, hands-on workshop on ASP.NET Core architecture
> that consulting firms are using in two ways:
>
> **Internally:** To get consultants up to speed on modern patterns before
> client engagements—reducing ramp-up time and improving delivery quality.
>
> **For clients:** As a training service that generates revenue and opens doors
> for larger engagements.
>
> One firm I work with now offers this to every client starting a new .NET
> project. It's become their standard 'kickoff'
> service—$8,000 in training revenue that leads to $50,000+ implementation
> contracts."

### Qualifying Questions (60 seconds)

> "A few questions to see if there's a fit:
>
> 1. Does your firm do .NET development work for clients?
> 2. How do you currently handle training—both for your consultants and for
     clients?
> 3. Are you looking more for internal capability building, a client-facing
     service, or both?"

### Partnership Tiers (45 seconds)

> "We have a few partnership levels depending on what you need:
>
> **Referral Partner:** You introduce us to clients, we deliver, you earn 15-20%
> commission. No upfront investment.
>
> **Reseller Partner:** You sell training to clients, we deliver under your
> brand. You control the relationship.
>
> **White Label License:** Full rights to deliver yourself—internally and to
> clients. Your branding, your trainers.
>
> Which of those sounds closest to what you'd want?"

### The Ask (30 seconds)

**If qualified:**

> "It sounds like [Reseller/White Label/etc.] might be a good fit. The next step
> would be a 30-minute call where I can walk you through the curriculum and show
> you how other firms are positioning this with clients. Does [Day] at [Time]
> or [Day] at [Time] work?"

**If not qualified:**

> "Based on what you've shared, this might not be the right timing. Can I send
> you some information and check back in [timeframe]?"

### Handling Objections

**"We have our own trainers."**

> "That's great—the White Label or Train-the-Trainer option might be perfect
> then. You'd get the complete curriculum, instructor guide, and all materials.
> Your trainers deliver it. Would that be more interesting?"

**"Our clients don't pay for training."**

> "Some firms position it as part of project kickoff—'architecture alignment'
> rather than 'training.' Clients see it as reducing project risk. The framing
> makes a difference. Would it help to see how other firms pitch it?"

**"We're not sure there's demand."**

> "That's fair. The Referral Partner model has zero upfront cost—you just make
> introductions and earn commission on anything that closes. It's a low-risk way
> to test the market. Would that work as a starting point?"

**"The pricing seems high."**

> "I understand. The typical ROI for clients is 5-10x in the first year from
> reduced ramp-up time and fewer architectural rewrites. Would it help to see the
> ROI calculation we use with clients?"

**"I need to discuss with partners/leadership."**

> "Absolutely. Can I send you a one-pager that summarizes the partnership
> options? And when should I follow up—would early next week work?"

### Closing

> "I'll send you a calendar invite for [Date/Time] and a brief overview of the
> partnership tiers. Is there anything specific you'd want me to prepare for that
> call—like case studies or pricing details?"

## Quick Reference: Key Talking Points

### Revenue Model for Consulting Firms

**Direct Training Revenue:**

- Charge clients $8,000-10,000 per workshop
- Deliver 4-6 workshops/year = $40,000-60,000 annual revenue

**Land-and-Expand:**

- Training leads to implementation contracts
- Average follow-on engagement: $50,000-150,000

**Internal ROI:**

- Faster consultant ramp-up = higher utilization rates
- Consistent delivery quality = better client satisfaction

### How to Position with Clients

> "Before we start the implementation, we recommend a one-day architecture
> alignment session for your team. This ensures everyone understands the patterns
> we'll use, reduces miscommunication during development, and cuts your code
> review time in half. Teams that do this have 40% fewer false starts on new
> projects."

### Competitive Differentiation

> "Your competitors send proposals. You send proposals with a training component
> that demonstrates technical depth before the project even starts. That's how you
> win deals on expertise, not just price."
